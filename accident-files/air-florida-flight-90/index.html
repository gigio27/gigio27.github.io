Air Florida Flight 90

Washington, 13 January 1982. A Boeing 737 took off in a snowstorm, stalled and struck the 14th Street Bridge before falling into the icy Potomac. 78 people died and 5 survived.

Date
13 January 1982, about 16:01 local time (EST)
Place
14th Street Bridge and Potomac River, Washington, D.C., about three quarters of a mile from the end of runway 36 at Washington National Airport
Aircraft
Boeing 737-222, registration N62AF
Flight
Washington National to Fort Lauderdale, with a stop in Tampa
Crew
Captain Larry Wheaton, First Officer Roger Pettit (the pilot flying)
Deaths
70 of the 74 passengers, 4 of the 5 crew, and 4 people on the bridge
Investigation
NTSB, Aircraft Accident Report AAR-82-8

What happened

Snow closed Washington National Airport from 13:38 to 14:53. Flight 90 left more than an hour and a half late. The 737 was de-iced, then waited 49 minutes in the line of aircraft waiting to take off while snow kept falling, and the crew did not go back for a second de-icing. The engine anti-ice was off.

The takeoff thrust target was an engine pressure ratio (EPR) of 2.04, and the gauges showed 2.04. With ice blocking the engine inlet probes, they were wrong: the engines were producing about 1.70. The 737 needed 45 seconds and 5,400 feet of runway to lift off instead of the usual 30 seconds and 3,500 feet. It climbed to about 350 feet, stalled, and hit the northbound span of the bridge, then the river.

The gauges that misled the crew

On the 737's engines, thrust is read as EPR. A blocked inlet probe reads the wrong pressure, so the gauge can show the takeoff value while the engine delivers much less. The first officer noticed something was wrong almost at once, as the recorder shows below. The NTSB faulted the captain for not rejecting the takeoff in its early stage, when there was still plenty of time and runway to stop.

Timeline

  1. 13:38Washington National Airport closes for snow removal. It reopens at 14:53.
  2. About 14:50 to 15:10The 737 is de-iced.
  3. 15:25A tug without chains cannot push the aircraft back on the snow. The crew tries reverse thrust for 30 to 90 seconds; the tug operator said this was against company policy.
  4. 15:35 to 15:39Pushback with another tug, then the 737 joins the line of aircraft waiting to take off.
  5. 15:59:28Cleared for takeoff and told not to delay, with landing traffic 2.5 miles out.
  6. 16:00:39The stall warning starts, two seconds after the captain calls V2.
  7. About 16:01The 737 strikes the 14th Street Bridge, hitting six cars and a truck, and falls into the Potomac.

Probable cause, in the NTSB's words

"The National Transportation Safety Board determines that the probable cause of this accident was the flightcrew's failure to use engine anti-ice during ground operation and takeoff, their decision to take off with snow/ice on the airfoil surfaces of the aircraft, and the captain's failure to reject the takeoff during the early stage when his attention was called to anomalous engine instrument readings."

"Contributing to the accident were the prolonged ground delay between deicing and the receipt of ATC takeoff clearance during which the airplane was exposed to continual precipitation, the known inherent pitchup characteristics of the B-737 aircraft when the leading edge is contaminated with even small amounts of snow or ice, and the limited experience of the flightcrew in jet transport winter operations."

NTSB, Aircraft Accident Report AAR-82-8

In the river

Five people survived. A US Park Police helicopter reached the scene around 16:20, and a bystander, Roger Olian, went into the river to help. A passenger, Arland Williams, helped others to be rescued and drowned. Thirteen days later, President Reagan paid tribute to the rescue in his State of the Union address.

What changed

  • On 28 January 1982, fifteen days after the crash, the NTSB issued ten safety recommendations, the first of them about plugged engine probes. Eleven more followed on 11 August 1982.
