A documentary a night, made by an AI agent

I built an engine that researches, writes, fact-checks, illustrates, narrates and uploads a twelve-minute documentary every night, on a laptop. It has made 93 films in under three months. I'm thinking of packaging it as a kit. Would you want it?

What it does, every night

  1. Picks a subject from what is already working on other channels, ranked by how far a video beat its own channel's median, not by raw views.
  2. Researches it from real sources and stores their text.
  3. Writes the script, then checks every number, date and name against those stored sources. A claim with no source fails the run. Three failures and the topic is dropped for good.
  4. Plans 60 to 120 shots and fetches public-domain and CC BY archives (US National Archives, Library of Congress, NASA, Wikimedia Commons). Share-alike and non-commercial licences are refused in code, and a small vision model looks at each picture before it is used.
  5. Narrates, renders with ffmpeg one scene at a time (resumable, cached per scene), and adds subtitles.
  6. Uploads, then pulls the retention curves every day and turns them into rules for the next scripts.

What the kit would contain

  • The Claude Code skills that run the night: subject, research, script, fact check, scene plan.
  • The Python engine: archive routing with licence checks, long-form ffmpeg assembly, YouTube upload and analytics collection.
  • The night routine and its guardrails: budget caps, anti-template rules, the fact-check gate.
  • A written walkthrough of the architecture, and of the mistakes that cost the most.

What it is not

Not a promise of views or income. The channel it runs has 51 subscribers. YouTube does not pay for AI content that looks mass-produced, and no kit changes that. This is an engineering kit for people who want to build and understand their own pipeline.
